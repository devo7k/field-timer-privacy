Field Timer Privacy Policy

Effective 2 November 2026

In short: Field Timer uses your location only to record when you leave or arrive at your hotels and work sites, to find those places for you, and to note which country you are in each day. Your times and places are stored only on your phone. The app has no account and no server. It goes online only for what you choose: downloading street maps, and asking Google for a place’s name if you allow it. You can delete your data at any time, and deleted data is erased.

This policy explains what the Field Timer app (“the app”) does with your information. The app is made by mcher.

What the app uses and why

  • Precise location, including in the background. When you turn on automatic tracking, your phone’s built-in geofencing tells the app when you cross the edge of a place you saved, even when the app is closed or not in use. The app uses this only to record the time and whether you left or arrived.
  • Place finding (Android, can be turned off). While tracking is on and “Find places automatically” is on, the phone gives the app its position a few times an hour. The app uses it only to notice places where you stayed for hours (a night, a work day) and to suggest them as your hotel or a work site. You decide whether to add them. The app does not keep a list of positions, so it never has your route. Until the app has processed a position, Android may hold it briefly in the system’s job queue on the phone.
  • Country for each day. The app works out which country you are in from built-in country borders, on the phone, using your saved places and the place-finding positions. It keeps only the date and the country.
  • Your current location, once. When you tap “Use my current location” while saving a place, the app reads your position one time to fill in that place’s coordinates.
  • Your current location on the Today map. While the Today tab is on screen, the app reads your position to show it as a dot on today’s map. It is not saved and stops when you leave the tab or the app.
  • Notifications (optional, off by default). If you turn on the status notification, the app shows where you are and today’s times in your notification bar. It is created on your phone from the app’s own data, its text is hidden on a locked screen, and it is removed when you turn it off or delete all data. The app does not use push messages.

The app asks for permission before using location, and explains why first. You can turn location access off at any time in your phone’s settings. You can still enter times by hand without it.

What is stored, and where

The app stores the following only in its private storage on your phone:

  • The places you save: a name, coordinates, a radius, and whether the place is watched.
  • Recorded events: a time, whether you left or arrived, the place name, and whether it was detected automatically or entered by hand. Events do not contain coordinates.
  • Place finding: the stay in progress (its centre and start and end times, one record that is overwritten), the places found until you add or dismiss them, and spots you said not to suggest, rounded to about 100 m. Turning place finding off erases all of it.
  • Countries: a date and a country code for each day. You can delete a day, a stay, a country or all of them on the Countries tab; turning “Record countries” off erases them too. Deleting times keeps the countries.
  • Your settings, such as when the week starts.

What the app does not do

  • It does not send your data off your phone. It has no server and no account.
  • It has no advertising, analytics, tracking or crash-reporting tools.
  • It does not look up addresses or places online. Day maps and the names suggested for places found come from a map file on your phone. You can import that file, or let the app download it.
  • It does not sell, rent or share your data with anyone.
  • It does not use your location for any purpose other than recording your arrival and departure times, finding your hotel and work sites, and noting the country for each day.

Google services (optional, Android)

The app asks once whether it may use Google services; you can change your answer on the Privacy tab. If you allow it, the app asks Google for the name of a hotel or work site: when it suggests a place it found, and when you tap “Use my current location” while adding a place. For that, the position of that one place is sent to Google (Google Places, or the address service built into Android). Nothing else is sent: no times, no other places, no account. Google’s privacy policy applies to what Google receives. If you do not allow it, the app names places from the map on your phone and does not contact Google.

Google Play's installer check

The copy from Google Play includes an installer check added by Google, which checks that the app came from Google Play. The app gives it none of your times, places or location.

Map downloads (optional)

If you tap “Download map for my area”, or turn on “Download automatically”, the app downloads street map data (OpenStreetMap, published by Protomaps) for the 25 km squares of a fixed grid that contain where you are (for automatic downloads, only while tracking is on) and your hotel and work site. Several maps can be kept on the phone; a downloaded map not used for 30 days is deleted by itself, and you can remove any map yourself. The map server sees your phone’s internet address and which squares are downloaded, as any website sees its visitors. It is not told your position within those squares, and the app sends no other data: no account, no device ID, no times, no place names. Automatic downloads happen while the app is open, on Wi-Fi, or also on mobile data if you turn on “Also on mobile data”. Protomaps’ own privacy practices apply to their server.

Exports

When you tap “Export week”, the app creates a spreadsheet file with that week’s times and place names. It does not include coordinates. Your phone’s share screen opens, and you choose where the file goes, for example your email. The app does not send it anywhere by itself. After you share it, the app or service you chose handles the file under its own privacy policy. The temporary file is deleted from the app the next time you open it, export again, or delete all data.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays on your phone until you delete it. The developer holds no copy of it. The app cannot delete what Google or the map server already received if you used those options, such as the position of a place or a map request; their own policies apply to it. In the app you can:

  • delete a single recorded time,
  • delete all times for a week (the countries for those days are kept),
  • delete times older than a period you choose, automatically (countries are kept),
  • delete the country of a single day, a stay, a whole country for a year, or all countries,
  • delete a place, which erases its coordinates, and
  • delete all data, which stops tracking and erases the app’s database and files, including all offline maps.

To delete all your Field Timer data:

  1. Open Field Timer and go to the Privacy tab.
  2. Tap “Delete all data”.
  3. Tap “Delete everything” to confirm.

This deletes every recorded time, every place, the places found, the countries, the settings and all offline maps from the phone. The app keeps nothing, and the developer has nothing to delete, because the app sends nothing to the developer.

Deletion is permanent. Deleted records are overwritten, not just hidden. Uninstalling the app also removes all of its data.

Backups. On Android, the app is excluded from Google’s automatic cloud backup, so its data is not copied to Google Drive. On iPhone, device backups to iCloud or a computer include app data, as they do for every app. After you delete data in the app, new backups no longer contain it. Older backups keep it until they are replaced.

Children

The app is a work tool intended for adults. It is not directed at children, and it does not knowingly handle information from children.
